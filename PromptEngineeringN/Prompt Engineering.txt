Prompt Engineering:
=>Practicing of crafting inputs to send to llm so we get accurate, required and relevant output.
=>treat prompt engineering as programming language in natural languages.
=>Quality of input is directly proportional to quality of output
=>reduces time of iterations{back and forth}
without good prompt-> small rephrased question provides different results when we are expecting same result.
that's why for consistent and reproducible result for varied tasks we need prompt engineering.

Prompt=> toekn=> context[token that fits]=> Pattern Matching[predicting the next possible tokens]

Techniques of Prompting:
1) Zero shot prompting:
	Don't provide examples to AI, don't vague prompting but keep it simple
	Useful for simple well defined tasks, standard format questions, common knowledge questions
2) Few shots prompting:
	Provide an example to AI and tell it learn the behaviour from that example, now with this example our result will be a bit more accurate.
	Useful for domain specific questions{example car related questions but llm doesn't know which model of car}, custom styles and zero shot gives inconsistent result.
3) chain of thought:
	Useful when you want AI to do logical thinking, make AI think step by step.
	when you want solve to your debugging issues, DSA , tasks where decision making is very important and consistent and improves or enhances retrieval and reasoning quality.
4) Role respective:
	Useful when you want AI to think as per that role,
	try an example for finding differences in response between assume ai you're an architect in cloud engineering with this much yoe vs no yoe cloud architect and see the trade offs in 	architecture, depth in explanation, perspective answers and vocabulary.
	Here also expert advice, audience appropriate answers if we are looking for such responses.
5)System prompts:
  Now when you develop system prompts we need to think it as itself a model with phases:
	1) Role[let AI know what it is]
	2) tone[how you and AI communicates]
	3) format[Always ask how your response should be]
	4) boundaries[advice -> pros and cons what to tell what it shouldn't tell]
	5) Knowledge[domain reference data based on this data I'm providing this response]
	
6) Output Formatting:
	Very important when building AI systems and workflows
	Useful for writing in Structured lists, tables, when you want to control character or word limit/ responses in our custom templates.

**Iterative refinement: Always try to refine your prompts as the prompts generated can be both good or bad. Even bad prompts can be remade into good with iterative refinement.
The cycle should be like:
	Write[write your prompt]-> run[test with ai]->evaluate[generate output]-> identify[find gaps of errors]->refine[describe details/constraints]-> back to write

**Prompt Chaining: Breaking complex to sequence of tasks where every output feeds to the next
	Resource[list key points]->outline[overall structure]->draft[first version]->edit[refine and polish]->review[proofread and verify]

Advanced Techniques:
	Tree of thought: we make sure AI walks in every path possible and after that we ask llm for an optional solution when next time we provide same prompts it reduces iterations 	needed.
	-> Useful when creative problem questions, when there are multiple valid approached but you want an optimal solution, strategic planning in your org.
	deliberate across paths rather than going with first response itself.


Now what to avoid during prompting:
	-> Avoid vague prompting, always go with few shots prompting technique.
	->Don't overload prompts[one task per prompt]
	-> ask llm to provide respect in respected output format.
	-> Provide a small context or summary about what you are asking.
	-> Don't accept the very first response from llm, try to refine your prompt for better result.
